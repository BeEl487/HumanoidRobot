## Summary: what this session's investigation resolved

`place_success_rate` was ~0% (one blip, pp_v8) for the entire project's history through pp_v21 --
roughly 20M+ cumulative training steps across 21 runs, including 5 individually-verified reward
fixes on the same continuation lineage (pp_v17-v21) that produced zero measurable change on the
exact metric each targeted. That flat non-response, combined with physics/actuator/mass checks
ruling out a physical cause, pointed at a policy-weight-level "reinforced habit" rather than a
reward-shape problem. A fresh-init run with a properly rebalanced curriculum (pp_v22, giving a
from-scratch policy real exposure to the full task chain instead of the 10% full-task rate that
sank the first fresh-init attempt, pp_v20) broke the ceiling completely and was then compounded
across four further continuations (pp_v23-v26) to a stable **~60-70% place success, 100%
lift-given-attach**. See `TRAINING_LOG.md`'s pp_v16→pp_v26 sections for the full diagnostic trail
(scripted verification, IK reachability checks, step-by-step trace logs, 30-episode diagnostics at
every checkpoint) and the "Lessons learned" section for the reusable patterns this surfaced.

**Note on pp_v8's curriculum continuation:** `--init-checkpoint` restores policy/value network
weights and optimizer state, but `start_attached_prob` lives in `config/pick_place_env.yaml`, read
fresh at each run's start -- so pp_v8 restarted the anneal from `start_attached_prob_initial: 0.6`
rather than continuing from wherever pp_v7's anneal had reached by step 1.2M. Not necessarily wrong
(more curriculum-assisted practice on the new `cube_disturbance` penalty isn't unreasonable), but
worth knowing: checkpoint continuation currently carries over network weights only, not
